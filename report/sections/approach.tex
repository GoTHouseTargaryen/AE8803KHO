\section{Approach}

\subsection{Time-Expanded Network and DP Formulation}

The assembly campaign is modeled as a finite-horizon Markov
decision process over a time-expanded network. Decision epochs are
discretized into configurable periods of length $\Delta t$
(default: 7 days). The system state at epoch $t$ is

\begin{equation}
  S(t) = \bigl\{\mathcal{M}(t),\; \mathcal{C}(t),\;
                \mathcal{Q}(t),\; n_{\mathrm{tugs}}(t)\bigr\}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $\mathcal{M}(t)$ is the set of completed modules,
$\mathcal{C}(t)$ is the set of docked crew vehicles with their
remaining rotation periods, $\mathcal{Q}(t)$ is the cargo-in-transit
queue with arrival times, and $n_{\mathrm{tugs}}(t)$ is the number
of reusable transfer stages available at LEO.

At each epoch the optimizer selects a decision
$D(t) = \{\text{launches},\;\text{crew allocation},\;
\text{assembly tasks},\;\text{tug assignments}\}$
that transitions the system to state $S'$. The Bellman optimality
equation is

\begin{equation}
  V(S,t) = \min_{D \in \mathcal{D}(S,t)}
            \bigl[\, c(S, D, t) + V(S', t+1) \,\bigr]
\end{equation}

\subsection{Multi-Objective Cost Function}

Three objectives are minimized simultaneously via a weighted sum
with safe normalization:

\begin{equation}
  J = w_1 \frac{N_{\mathrm{launches}}}{N_{\mathrm{launches}}^{\max}}
    + w_2 \frac{T}{T^{\max}}
    + w_3 \frac{C}{C^{\max}}
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}

\noindent where weights $w_1, w_2, w_3 \geq 0$ are user-adjustable via
sliders in the web interface.
Upper bounds used for safe normalization are
$N_{\mathrm{launches}}^{\max} = 2|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{total}}|$,
$T^{\max} = T_{\mathrm{horizon}}$ (default 200 periods), and
$C^{\max} = \$2{,}000|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{total}}|$\,M.
The $C^{\max}$ ceiling exceeds any single mid-size-vehicle launch cost
(e.g.\ \$150\,M for Falcon Heavy) while remaining below the total
expenditure of a large all-SLS campaign
($\$4{,}100\,\mathrm{M}\times |\mathcal{M}|$), ensuring that the
normalized cost term stays in the range $[0,1]$ for representative
mission architectures.
Safe normalization returns 0 when the upper bound is zero.

\subsection{Transfer Model}

Payload mass delivered to L4 is computed via the Tsiolkovsky
rocket equation applied to the LEO-to-L4 transfer:

\begin{equation}
  m_{\mathrm{delivered}} = m_{\mathrm{LEO}}
    \cdot \exp\!\left(\frac{-\Delta v}{I_{sp}\, g_0}\right)
  \label{eq:rocket}
\end{equation}

\noindent with $g_0 = 9.807 \times 10^{-3}$\,km/s$^2$. Two
transfer options are modeled: a direct Hohmann-like transfer
($\Delta v = 4.1$\,km/s, 60-day transit) and a low-energy
weak-stability-boundary (WSB) transfer ($\Delta v = 3.8$\,km/s,
120-day transit)~\cite{belbruno_wbs}.

For LEO-only vehicles that require a transfer stage of dry mass
$m_d$ and propellant mass $m_p$, the tug mass ratio $R =
\exp(\Delta v / I_{sp} g_0)$ constrains maximum deliverable cargo:

\begin{align}
  m_{\mathrm{cargo,max}} &= \frac{R\,m_d - (m_d + m_p)}{1 - R}
  \label{eq:tug} \\
  m_{\mathrm{delivered}} &= \min\!\left(m_{\mathrm{payload}},\;
                               m_{\mathrm{cargo,max}}\right)
\end{align}

\subsection{Crew Model}

All crew across docked vehicles form a single shared pool:

\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{crew}} = \sum_{v \in \mathcal{C}(t)} n_v
\end{equation}

EVA operations require a buddy system (pairs) and intravehicular
activity (IVA) monitors. The stable allocation is found
iteratively:

\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{IVA}} = \left\lceil
    \frac{n_{\mathrm{EVA\,pairs}}}{k_{\mathrm{IVA}}}
  \right\rceil, \quad
  n_{\mathrm{EVA\,pairs}} = \left\lfloor
    \frac{n_{\mathrm{crew}} - n_{\mathrm{IVA}}}{2}
  \right\rfloor
  \label{eq:eva}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $k_{\mathrm{IVA}}$ is the maximum EVA pairs per
IVA monitor (default: 2). Productive assembly hours per period
are reduced by the duty cycle $\eta_d = 0.6$ to account for
rest and maintenance overhead:

\begin{equation}
  H_{\mathrm{EVA}} = n_{\mathrm{EVA\,pairs}} \cdot h_{\mathrm{session}}
                     \cdot d_{\mathrm{period}} \cdot \eta_d
  \label{eq:crew_hours}
\end{equation}

Robotic arm stations operate continuously (24/7) but with a
time penalty $\eta_r = 1.5$ relative to crew EVA:

\begin{equation}
  H_{\mathrm{robotic}} = \frac{n_{\mathrm{arms}} \cdot h_{\mathrm{period}}}{\eta_r}
\end{equation}

Each crew vehicle trades unused crew capacity for cargo mass:

\begin{equation}
  m_{\mathrm{cargo}} = m_{\mathrm{capacity}}
                       - n_{\mathrm{crew}} \cdot m_{\mathrm{per\,crew}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Proximity Operations and Congestion Model}

Simultaneous vehicle presence at the assembly site increases
coordination overhead, reducing effective assembly throughput.
A scale-adaptive penalty function is applied to available crew
and robotic hours each period:

\begin{equation}
  \pi(n,\,p) = 1 + \frac{\alpha\,(n-1)^{\beta}}{C(p)}, \quad
  C(p) = C_{\mathrm{base}} + (C_{\mathrm{max}} - C_{\mathrm{base}})\,p
  \label{eq:penalty}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $n$ is the number of vehicles in proximity
(crew vehicles plus arriving cargo vehicles), $p =
|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{built}}| / |\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{total}}|$
is fractional build progress, and default parameters are
$\alpha = 0.1$, $\beta = 1.5$, $C_{\mathrm{base}} = 2$,
$C_{\mathrm{max}} = 10$. Effective assembly hours are:

\begin{equation}
  H_{\mathrm{EVA,eff}} = \frac{H_{\mathrm{EVA}}}{\pi(n,p)}, \quad
  H_{\mathrm{robotic,eff}} = \frac{H_{\mathrm{robotic}}}{\pi(n,p)}
\end{equation}

Cumulative collision risk is tracked as a safety metric:

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R} = \sum_{t=0}^{T} \kappa \cdot \binom{n(t)}{2} \cdot \Delta t
  \label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Partial Assembly (Work-in-Progress)}

Modules whose assembly hours exceed one period's capacity are
carried forward as work-in-progress (WIP). Remaining hours
$h_r$ are updated each period:

\begin{equation}
  h_r^{(t+1)} = \max\!\left(0,\;
    h_r^{(t)} - \min\!\left(h_r^{(t)},\; H_{\mathrm{available}}^{(t)}\right)
  \right)
\end{equation}

\noindent A module is marked complete when $h_r \leq \varepsilon$
(default $\varepsilon = 0.01$\,h).

\subsection{Vehicle Availability and Lead-Time Cost}

Each launch vehicle $v$ carries a \emph{lead time}
$\tau_v \geq 0$ (in periods) representing the minimum elapsed
campaign time before its first launch is permitted. This
captures procurement contracts, launch-site integration,
nuclear certification, and export licensing:

\begin{equation}
  t \;\geq\; \tau_v,
  \quad \forall\, v \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{cargo}} \cup
  \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{crew}}
  \label{eq:leadtime}
\end{equation}

\noindent Vehicles are classified by readiness status:
\emph{Operational} vehicles (Falcon Heavy, SLS Block~2, Vulcan Centaur,
Ariane~6, GSLV Mk~III, H3) have $\tau_v = 4$--26 periods
(1--6 calendar months); \emph{Near-term} vehicles (Starship, New Glenn,
Starship HLS) have $\tau_v = 13$--26 periods (3--6 months); and
\emph{In-development} vehicles (KSLV-III) have
$\tau_v = 78$ periods ($\sim$18 months).

Contracting, integration, and mission-assurance work
incurred during the lead-time window constitutes a
one-time overhead. This is modeled as a
\emph{first-flight cost premium} $\delta_v \in [0,1]$
applied only to the first launch of vehicle $v$:

\begin{equation}
  C_v^{(k)} =
  \begin{cases}
    c_v\bigl(1 + \delta_v\bigr) & k = 1 \\
    c_v                         & k > 1
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:firstflight}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $c_v$ is the nominal per-launch cost and
$k$ is the launch index for that vehicle. Premium values
range from $\delta_v = 0.08$ (operational, established
manifest) to $\delta_v = 0.40$ (in-development, full
qualification amortized over one flight). The total
program cost accumulated in Eq.~(\ref{eq:cost}) uses
$C_v^{(k)}$ in place of $c_v$.

\subsection{Pad Turnaround Constraint}

Each launch vehicle $v$ is subject to a minimum inter-launch
interval to model launch pad refurbishment:

\begin{equation}
  t - t_{\mathrm{last}}(v) \geq \Delta t_{\mathrm{pad}},
  \quad \forall\, v \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{cargo}} \cup
  \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{crew}}
\end{equation}

\noindent Default: $\Delta t_{\mathrm{pad}} = 2$ periods (14 days).

\subsection{Beam Search Pruning}

Exact DP over the assembled module bitmask is intractable for
structures with $|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{total}}| \geq 20$
($2^{50}$ states for a 50-module spacecraft). Forward beam search
retains only the top-$K$ states per epoch. States are ranked by a
three-tier lexicographic key $\mathbf{k}(S)$:

\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{k}(S) = \bigl(
    -|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{built}}|,\;
    -\psi(S),\;
    J(S)
  \bigr)
  \label{eq:beamsort}
\end{equation}

\noindent sorted in ascending order (lower is better).
The secondary score $\psi(S)$ blends pipeline depth with the
weighted objective cost:

\begin{equation}
  \psi(S) =
  \begin{cases}
    0
      & \text{if idle}(S) \\[4pt]
    \max\!\bigl(\,\phi(S)
               + 2\,|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{total}}|
               - J(S)\cdot|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{total}}|,\;1\bigr)
      & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}

\noindent where a state is \emph{idle} if $\mathcal{Q} = \emptyset$
(no cargo in transit), $\mathcal{D} = \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{built}}$
(no modules at site awaiting assembly), and no work-in-progress
remains. The pipeline-depth heuristic $\phi(S)$ is:

\begin{equation}
  \phi(S) = 3\,|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{built}}|
          + 2\,|\mathcal{D} \setminus \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{built}}|
          + |\mathcal{Q}|
          + \sum_{v \in \mathcal{C}(t)} r_v
  \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $r_v$ is the periods remaining in crew vehicle $v$'s
rotation and $J(S)$ is the current weighted cost (Eq.~\ref{eq:cost}).

The three tiers serve distinct roles.
The \emph{primary tier} ($-|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{built}}|$) is a
hard completion guarantee: states that have assembled more modules
are never discarded in favour of cheaper but idle states.
The \emph{secondary tier} $\psi(S)$ has two responsibilities.
First, the activity bonus $2|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{total}}|$ ensures
that any state with active pipeline ($\psi \geq 1$) always outranks
a completely idle state ($\psi = 0$), preventing the beam from
filling with do-nothing trajectories when cost or launch-count
weights are large.
Second, subtracting $J(S)\cdot|\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{total}}|$ means
trajectories that accumulate high objective cost—for example, those
that launch an expensive vehicle during a cheaper vehicle's pad
turnaround cooldown—are penalized in tier~2, making the weight
sliders effective even when the pad constraint forces an immediate
scheduling choice between a cheap vehicle on cooldown and an
expensive vehicle that is currently eligible.
The \emph{tertiary tier} $J(S)$ resolves any remaining ties by pure
objective value. Default beam width: $K = 100$.
